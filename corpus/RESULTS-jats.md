# Corpus coverage: scholarly articles (third genre)

Generated by `tools/jats.py` over 14 open-access articles in JATS XML, from Europe PMC.

This genre exists to reach what the first two could not. Administrative notices and standards documents contain no images and no footnotes, which left `media`, `note` and `note-ref` resting on the author's judgement rather than on evidence. Scholarly articles have all three in quantity.

## 3. Accessibility, measured against reality

`media.alt` is a required, non-empty field, so **a figure with no alternative text is unrepresentable**. That is the accessibility claim stated as a property of the format rather than as a policy. This corpus is the first chance to see what it costs against real documents.

- Figures encountered: **41**
- Graphics encountered: **65**
- Graphics carrying `<alt-text>` in the source: **0** (0%)
- Graphics with **no** alternative text in the source: **65** (100%)

Every one of those would pass an HTML validator, ship, and fail a reader. Here they cannot be expressed at all: the mapper had to derive alternative text from the figure caption before a conforming document existed. The derived text is not as good as authored alternative text — the point is that *the absence is impossible*, so the failure happens at build time, in front of the author, instead of silently in front of a reader.

- Footnotes carried as `note` with a `note-ref` marker: **48**
