# Corpus coverage: public-sector forms (L2)

Generated by `tools/forms.py` over 10 public-sector pages carrying 16 expressible forms.

The other three corpora are static documents, which cannot contain a form, so L2 was recorded as unfalsifiable. Public bodies publish forms constantly, so that was an excuse rather than a limit. This measures it.

## 1. Coverage

**100%** (10/10) of pages produce a conforming document whose L0 projection also conforms.

## 2. Labels: the accessibility claim applied to interaction

`field.label` is a required, non-empty field, so **an unlabelled control is unrepresentable**. There is no `<input>` with no label to write.

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Controls encountered | **2449** |
| With a real `<label>` (for= or wrapping) | **2446** (100%) |
| With **no** `<label>` at all | **3** (0%) |
| — rescued from `aria-label`/`title` | 1 |
| — rescued from `placeholder` | 0 |
| — **no label of any kind, unrepresentable** | **2** |

A placeholder is not a label: it disappears on focus and is not announced reliably. Counting those separately is the point — in HTML they pass, here they had to be promoted to a real label or dropped.

## 3. The fetch boundary applied to submission

`form.action` is typed `path`, so **a form cannot post across origins**. Not discouraged: unrepresentable.

- Form actions examined: **18**
- Naming another origin, therefore inexpressible: **0** (0%)

Still unexercised by real documents: `tab-group`, `tab`.

`tab-group` and `tab` are the honest problem: tabbed content on the public web is built with scripts, so a server-rendered corpus cannot reach them. They are two elements justified by nothing but the author's judgement, and the finite-vocabulary argument says that is a reason to remove them rather than to keep looking.
